Exoplanets are planets that orbit stars outside our solar system. Thousands have been confirmed, and scientists believe many more may exist. Astronomers often discover exoplanets by observing tiny changes in a star’s brightness when a planet passes in front of it. Other techniques measure how a planet’s gravity causes its star to move slightly. Researchers are especially interested in planets located in habitable zones, where temperatures could potentially allow liquid water. Finding a planet in this region does not prove that life exists there. Nevertheless, studying exoplanets helps scientists understand how common planetary systems are throughout the galaxy.
